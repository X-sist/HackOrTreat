### 3. Friction Points & 6-Hour Survival Rules

* **The Serial Baud Rate Bottleneck:** Arduino Serial communication and IR transmission operate at vastly different speeds. Ensure both Arduinos are set to a fast baud rate (e.g., `115200`), but make sure the Python/Node scripts actually wait for a newline character (`\n`) before processing. Do not flood the Arduino buffer from Laptop 1 faster than the IR LED can physically flash.
* **IR Ambient Noise:** Hackathon venues have terrible fluorescent lighting that can sometimes emit noise in the 38kHz range. The CE major must use a tightly rolled piece of paper or a 3D-printed shroud over the IR receiver to act as a physical bandpass filter, ensuring it only sees the direct beam from your transmitter.
* **The "Local Offline" Trap:** The moment you turn off Wi-Fi, APIs fail. Python libraries like `transformers` or `whisper` often try to ping HuggingFace to check for model updates, which will crash your script if it hangs on a network timeout. You must explicitly set environment variables (like `HF_HUB_OFFLINE=1`) tonight and run the full pipeline once completely disconnected from the internet to ensure weights are reading from the local disk cache.
* **Model Hallucination on Garbage Data:** When the IR beam is broken, the receiving Arduino might pass corrupted junk characters (e.g., `wh*t is y@ur n%me`) to the LLM. You must use a robust system prompt in Ollama commanding Qwen to ignore special characters, deduce the core intent, and prioritize reacting to the `<AETHER_DISTURBED>` tag if present in the string.
* **TTS Concurrency:** Piper TTS takes a moment to generate the audio file before playing it. Have your Node backend trigger the planchette animation to start spelling out the words visually *while* the audio is generating in the background to mask the latency.
